## Do they render? Yes — verified, not assumed

`tools/prop-audit.js` renders **every** imported prop alone against a magenta
background and measures the framebuffer: how much of the frame it covers, and how
many distinct colours it drew. That is the difference between "loaded without error"
and "actually renders", which have diverged repeatedly in this project.

| verdict | count | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `OK` | 634 | real geometry, more than one colour, sane size |
| `THIN` | 6 | a sliver — a paddle, a plank. Correct, just narrow |
| `FLAT` | 8 | one colour. Wrong atlas, or a genuinely single-swatch prop |
| `EMPTY` | 6 | nothing drew — a horizontal card seen edge-on |

### Animated wildlife (4)

Seagull, Crow and Fish are SKINNED meshes with animation. Their geometry arrives from FBXLoader without a usable position buffer, and three.js throws inside Box3.setFromObject before anything can measure or export them. Four props out of 658, all decorative — a wheeling bird is a nice touch, not a reason to build an animated-mesh path through the whole pipeline.

## How to change any of this

```
node tools/scene-manifest.js           # what resolves, what collides, what is missing
node tools/scene-manifest.js --audit   # the SKIP tally
node tools/scene-manifest.js --stage   # copy chosen FBX to tools/_fbx/
node tools/fbx2glb.js                  # convert, normalise size, decimate
node tools/prop-audit.js               # render every prop and verify
node tools/make-asset-doc.js           # regenerate this file
```

To promote something out of SKIP: move the family into a bucket in
`tools/scene-manifest.js`, then re-run the five commands above. The audit will tell
you immediately whether it renders.

One thing to know before adding anything: **`tools/_convert.html` holds a `SIZE`
table of real-world heights per family.** These six packs disagree about authoring
units by five orders of magnitude — `Mountain_1A` is 20,000 units and `Coconut_01A`
is 0.30 — so a new family without a `SIZE` entry falls back to 1 metre and will look
wrong. Add the height, not a scale factor.
